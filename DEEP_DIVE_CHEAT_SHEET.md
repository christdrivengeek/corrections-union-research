# Back-Pocket Deep Dive: Union vs. Non-Union Nuances

This document contains deep-dive research into specific employment conditions that are rarely publicized on basic salary charts. Use this as your "back-pocket" cheat sheet during the presentation when asked about the hidden value of the union contract.

## Contrast States Investigated:
We are actively auditing the detailed policy manuals and union contracts for 10 specific contrast states (5 Strong Union, 5 Strong Non-Union) regarding:
1. **Mandatory Overtime Rules & Penalties**
2. **Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay** (100% vs. Standard Workers Comp)
3. **Job Security** (Just Cause vs. At-Will)
4. **Seniority Rights** (Shift and Post Bidding)

## Arkansas (AR) - Non-Union (Right-to-Work)
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** No limits. Due to severe staffing shortages, officers are endlessly forced. Refusal is insubordination. There is no double-time penalty for forcing on days off, only standard FLSA time-and-a-half.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** Standard Workers' Comp only. If assaulted by an inmate, an officer's pay drops to roughly 66.67% of their average weekly wage. There is no 100% pay protection.
**3. Job Security:** Strictly "At-Will". The DOC can fire officers at any time without having to prove "Just Cause" to an arbitrator. Management can bypass progressive discipline entirely.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** None. Management prerogative. Shift bidding and post assignments are completely at the warden's discretion.

## Wyoming (WY) - Non-Union (Right-to-Work)
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** Frequently used and explicitly a condition of employment. Officers are often forced into double 16-hour shifts with no forewarning. Refusal to stay is a fireable offense.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** Standard Workers' Comp only. No special hazard pay for line-of-duty assaults; wage replacement drops to roughly two-thirds (66%).
**3. Job Security:** Strictly "At-Will". Officers do not hold a "property interest" in their jobs and can be terminated for any non-discriminatory reason without union representation or binding arbitration.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** None. The DOC explicitly dictates that officers must be available for any shift or post. Seniority is not an enforceable right for bidding on days off or preferred housing units.

## Idaho (ID) - Non-Union
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** Highly prevalent (16-hour shifts). The state frequently forces officers and compensates them with "comp time" instead of cash, which is notoriously difficult to use due to the short-staffing.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** *Exception to the rule:* Idaho actually has a specific statute (IC § 67-5340) that grants officers up to one year of leave without loss of salary if seriously injured by an inmate.
**3. Job Security:** Not purely at-will. They are "classified" state merit employees, meaning they require cause for termination. However, appeals go through the state, not an independent union arbitrator.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** Seniority is used in policy (SOP), but because there is no union contract, management retains the absolute right to override bids or change schedules for any operational need.

## Mississippi (MS) - Non-Union
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** Severe forced overtime. The state was famously sued for forcing officers to work and failing to pay cash overtime (giving them unusable comp time instead).
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** Standard Workers' Comp only. No 100% salary continuation for inmate assaults. You cannot sue the state for pain and suffering.
**3. Job Security:** "At-will" for the first 12 months. Afterwards, they gain "State Service" protection requiring cause, but appeals go to a state employee board rather than a neutral, binding arbitrator.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** None. There is no formalized, statewide policy guaranteeing seniority-based shift or post bidding. It is entirely at the warden's discretion.

## Massachusetts (MA) - Union (MCOFU)
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** Regulated by contract. While forced overtime exists due to 24/7 operations, the union contract (CBA) dictates the exact mathematical procedure for how it is assigned to prevent favoritism or targeting.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** 100% Full Pay. State law and the union contract guarantee that if assaulted by an inmate, the state supplements workers comp to equal exactly 100% of your regular salary without docking any sick leave until you recover or retire.
**3. Job Security:** Strict "Just Cause". Management must prove their case. If the union disagrees, they can take the state to binding arbitration where a neutral judge can overturn the termination.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** A core pillar of the contract. Allocation of shifts, days off, facility transfers, and "job picks" are awarded strictly by seniority.

## Texas (TX) - Non-Union
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** Widespread use. Frequently forced into double shifts with no double-time penalty.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** Standard Workers' Comp only (70-75%). Texas officers MUST burn their own accrued sick/vacation leave just to supplement the difference and get a full paycheck while recovering from an inmate assault.
**3. Job Security:** Strictly "At-Will". The Personnel Manual explicitly states employment is not a contract and officers can be fired at any time for any legal reason.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** None. Assignments are handled at the unit level entirely at the warden's discretion.

## Michigan (MI) - Union (MCO)
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** The "32-Hour Rule". The union contract restricts management from forcing an officer to work mandatory overtime within a 32-hour window of their last forced shift, giving them a guaranteed rest period.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** 100% Full Wages. State law (MCL 791.263a) championed by the union guarantees that if injured by a prisoner assault, the state supplements workers comp so the officer continues receiving full pay and fringe benefits.
**3. Job Security:** Strict "Just Cause". Includes powerful due-process rights like the right to view any video evidence of an incident *before* being interrogated by Internal Affairs.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** Historically heavily protected, though a 2017 Civil Service Commission ruling weakened it. However, the union still enforces localized policies heavily favoring seniority for shift preference.

## Minnesota (MN) - Union (AFSCME Unit 8)
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** Limits on forcing back-to-back. The contract penalizes the state with a $100 grievance payout directly to the officer if management violates the forcing order.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** 100% Full Pay. The AFSCME contract guarantees that if assaulted by an inmate, the state pays the difference between workers comp and full base salary, without deducting from the officer's accrued sick leave or vacation time (up to 240 hours).
**3. Job Security:** Strict "Just Cause". Protected from at-will firing. Disciplinary actions go to a neutral binding arbitrator.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** Strict "Classification Seniority". Your time-in-service dictates priority for bidding on vacant posts, shift selection, shift exchanges, and vacation time.

## Rhode Island (RI) - Union (RIBCO)
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** Extreme penalties. Due to staffing crises causing excessive forcing, recent union/legislative pushes require the state to pay **2.5x (Double time and a half)** for forced overtime that violates limits or falls on scheduled days off.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** Standard Workers' Comp. (Unlike MA or MI, the union is still fighting to get statutory 100% pay passed in the legislature).
**3. Job Security:** Strict "Just Cause". Arbitrators in RI frequently review cases and have the authority to overturn firings.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** Strong contractual seniority rights for bidding shifts, posts, and regular days off.

## Oregon (OR) - Union (AFSCME / AOCE)
**1. Mandatory Overtime:** Rotational bucket system to ensure fairness. Exemptions prohibit forcing an officer on their last shift before an approved vacation.
**2. Assault / Line-of-Duty Injury Pay:** 100% Full Pay. Contracts negotiate "Assault Pay" to supplement workers comp so the officer receives full take-home pay without draining sick leave.
**3. Job Security:** Strict "Just Cause" and binding arbitration.
**4. Seniority Bidding:** Seniority is the primary factor dictating an officer's schedule, standard posts, and preferred days off during regular bidding cycles.
